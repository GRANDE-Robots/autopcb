How AutoPCB makes a board

Follow a circuit from its pin and net definitions to the evidence needed for a manufacturing handoff.

What the solver actually decides

Circuit and mechanical constraints

A new design starts with an approved catalog pin-and-net map, footprints and size limits. A fixed size sets the outline directly. A maximum size lets the fitter test smaller rectangles. Imported projects start from their saved outline, copper and placement permissions.

Area and placement

The compact fitter tests the original poses and translations of permitted groups in two axis orders. It ranks candidates by compliance with the size limits, then area, then total movement. Bodies, pads, drills, courtyards and visible markings contribute to the envelope. The catalog floor bounds how small a successful board can get.

Routes and differential pairs

Heading-aware A* searches position and layer. Default local cost counts 1 unit per millimetre, an extra unit per diagonal millimetre, 10 per via and 1 per bend. Policy can add guide or layer costs. The catalog USB generator tries both members of a two-terminal pair together and checks launch geometry, spacing and length difference.

Repairs and protected geometry

Routing can try another checkpoint order, replace eligible copper or try an authorized bottom-side move within a private candidate. Connectivity runs require fewer opens. Dedicated cleanup modes can instead prove an angle correction, via reduction or dangling-copper repair at equal opens. Apply checks protected items, concurrent edits and editor locks.

Board score and electrical evidence

The board-level score orders eligible candidates by opens, symmetry and cost. Corresponding channels can be compared by route displacement, via alignment, bends and branching. The live connectivity loop publishes by verified open reduction. A tree model sums downstream currents and estimates voltage drop from supplied values. Ampacity, USB impedance and return-path exceptions need design-specific evidence.

Verification and handoff

Independent verification runs KiCad DRC, ERC and schematic parity and samples reference copper for declared differential pairs. Drafts include Gerbers, drills, BOM, placements, PDFs and findings. A checked package needs clean native checks and a source-bound electrical review that addresses the reference findings.

Inside the two reference circuits

I²C temperature sensor

The four header pins carry 3.3 V, ground, SDA and SCL, in that order. SDA and SCL each have a 4.7 kΩ pull-up. A 100 nF capacitor bypasses the MCP9808 supply. The sensor’s three address pins connect to ground and ALERT is intentionally open.

Full-speed USB Type-B breakout

The connector and header use the same pin order. Pins 1 through 4 carry VBUS, D−, D+ and ground. Both connector-shell tabs connect to ground. The compact board routes both data traces on the front over In1.Cu, with no signal vias. The return-copper screen flags all four data-pad launches.

Where to take placement next

Both compact designs reach their catalog size floor with the original component positions. A separate KiCad placement test starts the sensor and its three passives 11 mm farther right. AutoPCB translates the four together, keeps the header fixed and fits the 28 × 20 mm outline. The next case should require moving a group to open a routing corridor. Compare several permitted placements and keep the smallest fully routed candidate that passes the same checks.
